# Research Search Protocol: Preparing Students for the AI-Driven Labor Market

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines a transparent and reproducible approach for identifying, selecting, and synthesizing research on how artificial intelligence (AI) is changing the labor market and how educational institutions can prepare students for these changes.

It is intended to support the literature review and methodology of this research project. It should be refined as the research questions and available evidence develop.

## 2. Research Questions

The literature search will address the following questions:

1. How is AI affecting occupations, employment opportunities, and the tasks performed by workers?
2. Which technical, digital, analytical, and human skills are becoming important in an AI-enabled labor market?
3. How are schools, universities, and vocational institutions adapting their curricula and teaching methods?
4. What barriers limit students' access to AI education and relevant digital skills?
5. Which education and workforce-development strategies may help students prepare for future employment, particularly in developing economies?

## 3. Sources of Evidence

The research team should prioritize credible, traceable sources, including:

- Peer-reviewed academic journals and conference proceedings.
- Google Scholar and other academic discovery tools.
- Reports from the International Labour Organization (ILO).
- Publications from the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD).
- World Bank research and development reports.
- UNESCO publications on education and AI.
- World Economic Forum reports on jobs and skills.
- Official government, education-sector, and labor-market publications.
- Relevant institutional datasets with documented methods.

Search engines may help discover sources, but search-result summaries alone should not be treated as sufficient evidence. Researchers should consult the original publication whenever possible.

## 4. Search Strategy

Search terms should combine concepts related to AI, employment, skills, and education. Example queries include:

- "artificial intelligence" AND "future of work"
- "generative AI" AND employment AND skills
- AI AND occupational task exposure
- artificial intelligence AND higher education AND employability
- digital skills AND vocational education AND labor market
- AI adoption AND youth employment
- artificial intelligence AND workforce development AND Africa
- AI literacy AND curriculum AND students

Researchers should adapt search terms to each database and record the exact queries used. Where appropriate, searches should include synonyms and regional terms.

## 5. Inclusion Criteria

A source may be included when it:

- Directly addresses at least one research question.
- Comes from an identifiable and reasonably credible author or institution.
- Provides traceable evidence, analysis, or a clearly explained conceptual framework.
- Has sufficient methodological information for its findings to be assessed.
- Falls within the documented search period or provides essential historical context.
- Is available in a language the research team can reliably evaluate.

Recent publications should be prioritized for rapidly changing AI developments, while foundational research may be retained when relevant.

## 6. Exclusion Criteria

A source may be excluded when it:

- Is unrelated to the research questions.
- Makes unsupported claims without identifiable evidence.
- Is a duplicate of another record.
- Cannot be evaluated because essential information is missing.
- Is promotional material presented as independent research.
- Repeats claims from another source without adding useful evidence.

Exclusion decisions should be recorded with a brief reason. A source should not be rejected solely because its findings differ from the research team's expectations.

## 7. Screening and Selection

The selection process should follow these stages:

1. Identify records through documented searches.
2. Remove duplicate records.
3. Screen titles and abstracts or executive summaries.
4. Review the full text of potentially relevant sources.
5. Apply the inclusion and exclusion criteria.
6. Record the final selection and reasons for full-text exclusions.

The research team should report the number of records identified, screened, excluded, and included whenever these counts are available. The process should not be described as a systematic review unless the methods meet the relevant standards.

## 8. Data Extraction

For each included source, record the following information where available:

| Field | Information to record |
|---|---|
| Reference | Full citation, DOI, or stable URL |
| Publication | Author, institution, and year |
| Geography | Country or region studied |
| Research purpose | Main question or objective |
| Method | Dataset, sample, design, and analytical approach |
| AI focus | Technology or application examined |
| Labor-market outcomes | Jobs, tasks, wages, or employment effects |
| Skills | Skills identified as important |
| Education implications | Curriculum, teaching, or training recommendations |
| Limitations | Caveats identified by the authors |
| Relevance | Research question addressed |

Researchers should distinguish reported findings from their own interpretations and note missing information rather than inventing it.

## 9. Evidence Synthesis

The selected literature should be synthesized thematically around:

- Changes in occupations and work tasks.
- Emerging technical and transferable skills.
- Education and training responses.
- Access, equity, and digital infrastructure barriers.
- Regional differences, including evidence from African economies.
- Uncertainty, disagreement, and gaps in existing research.

The synthesis should compare findings across sources, consider differences in methods and contexts, and avoid assuming that AI will affect all occupations or countries in the same way.

## 10. Quality and Reliability Assessment

For each source, assess:

- Author and institutional credibility.
- Transparency of data and methods.
- Relevance and timeliness.
- Whether conclusions are supported by the evidence.
- Potential conflicts of interest.
- Limitations and uncertainty.

Institutional reputation alone should not replace an assessment of the underlying evidence. Forecasts and estimates should be clearly distinguished from observed outcomes.

## 11. Research Ethics and Responsible Use of AI

AI tools may assist with organizing records, developing search terms, or improving readability. Researchers must verify citations and factual claims against original sources.

AI-generated references must never be presented as verified publications without checking them. The research team should disclose material AI assistance where required by institutional or publication policies and respect copyright, privacy, and research-integrity requirements.

## 12. Reproducibility and Documentation

Maintain a search log containing:

- Search date.
- Database or website.
- Exact search query.
- Filters and date range.
- Number of records retrieved, where available.
- Screening decisions and exclusion reasons.
- Any changes to the protocol.

Save bibliographic records and extraction notes in a consistent format. Document amendments to this protocol with the date, reason, and description of the change.

## 13. Limitations

Search coverage may be incomplete because databases differ, some publications are inaccessible, and AI-related evidence changes rapidly. Search results may also favor English-language and higher-income-country research.

These limitations should be acknowledged in the final report. Findings should be interpreted according to the quality, geographic coverage, and limitations of the available evidence.

## 14. Expected Output

Applying this protocol should produce:

1. A documented set of relevant sources.
2. A structured evidence-extraction table.
3. A thematic synthesis linked to the research questions.
4. An account of evidence limitations and research gaps.
5. A reproducible record of the search and selection process.

This protocol is a proposed research procedure. It does not imply that searches have already been completed or that any findings have been established.
